AI creates an opportunity for task management software to become what it always dreamt of being. And it works both ways. Task management software is also an opportunity for AI to be closer to what we want to achieve.

I’m NOT talking about adding an AI assistant to a task tracker. I’m talking about understanding how and where AI helps when working towards a goal. And I'm talking about building the tool around it.

## The plan-work-plan loop opportunity
When working on its own, AI constantly goes from planning mode to doing the work. Planning and doing feed into each other. It's a marvel to see AI working on a complicated goal, constantly adjusting phases and tasks, reviewing and iterating. AI, with task management software, gives us the opportunity to work using a similarly tight plan-work-plan loop.

The "plan-work loop" is a lot more foundational and universal than it seems. Putting a bit more humanity into it, we could say "decide" instead of "plan", "do" instead of "work", and "take a step back" instead of "loop". So decide, do, and take a step back. Following these steps consistently is of course critical to reaching any goal.

In practice, it can be quite messy, though. I used to have a pretty severe case of scatterbrain. You may think everyone is a little bit like that. But people who know me sometimes call forgetting their passport or keys "doing a Matthieu", it was that bad. When starting on a new goal, I would have had a bit of my tracking in a todo app, maybe a spreadsheet, a bit in my head, another bit in a note-taking app, and maybe some on paper. I'd reach some sort of half-finished result, or lose sight of my original goal, letting it disappear from my everyday life. The "decide-do-take a step back" loop was taking the "take a step back" part too literally and leaving out the loop part entirely.

How can AI help us keep that focus and feel more accomplished?

## Making AI part of the work

Let's see what AI can help with:

1. Getting started. AI can draw from its close to infinite (and accurate-ish) knowledge to get an idea of how to reach any goal, and give a starting plan.
2. Figuring out difficult tasks. That's what we tend to already use AI for, and that's the biggest incentive we want for us to come back to our task manager. For example, what are good options for a shady flower bed? What are my options for room insulation? AI will help. 
3. Feedback. We all crave feedback, and AI is happy to deliver.
4. Coaching. What is the next important thing to work on? Maybe a little pep talk could also help with starting the day.
5. Consistent planning. Updating the plan to match reality is as valuable as it is painful. AI will adjust plans without an ounce of judgement.

AI chats already help with all of the above, sometimes with the help of MCP integration with task managers, or markdown to-do lists. Why is using AI from inside the task manager better?

The AI activities listed above are directly connected to the work and plan. The closer these activities are to what we've set out to do, the better. Coaching, getting expert help, and project management are simply going deeper into that original task management scope of planning and task tracking.

And of course, most task management systems have added contextualised AI to their offerings. Though their AI-task management integration flow is surprisingly basic. In particular, AI sessions and generated content are left to the users to organise and integrate in their workflow.

Maybe there's a way to better organise our messy AI sessions and generated content?

## Giving a space to AI

Finding a space for our AI conversations is a surprisingly unsolved subject. We have bits of AI conversations everywhere: ChatGPT, coding harnesses, in-app contextual help. Some sessions will have generated files used downstream. Some conversations will have back and forth resulting in decisions. Other conversations will contain a detailed generated tutorial to resolve a given task. 

If we happen to have a plan, a natural place for these AI conversation is within the relevant plan items.

Click on a task, and you get the related AI help. Besides being tidied in the right place, that help comes with the knowledge of the task, and the overall context of what you're trying to achieve, and all intermediate results, be it notes, documents or pictures. AI's response quality will only be as good as its context, and having access to the full goal picture is hard to beat.

Click on the top level goal item, and you're talking about the whole goal. Questions about planning, priorities, direction, overall design will naturally fit here.

Click on the home space, and you're now talking about what you want to do, today, or this week. Or what goals you want to reach.

There is a space for every conversation. Once you've seen and used AI-inside-the-plan, it just makes sense. And further opportunities start to appear.

## Furnishing the task space and making it a workspace

Let's push the idea of task space a little further. Nowadays, AI chats are evolving into workspaces. When working inside a task, the task you clicked is in effect that workspace, and it can be specialised for that particular task. In practice specialisation means AI skills, UI and features targeted at the task at hand. Existing use cases we see with ChatGPT and Claude are for example tooling to edit pictures, slides, or full design systems. 

Imagine having a task "refresh website design" in your plan. You click on it and automatically access specialised AI, tooling, and the relevant content.

Though we may want to use specialised software instead. For example, I want to use Canva because I'm used to it and already have existing assets I want to reuse. For tasks needing specialised software, the task workspace becomes the glue between the overall goal context and the app's capabilities. Taking Canva for example, one could directly create content from the task workspace. Or upload the relevant files and context into Canva workspace.

The task management app is no longer just a record of work to do. Our task management app becomes an active plan that is helping to do the work. And what we learn while doing the work feeds back into the plan: what is finished, what is blocked, and what should happen next.

## The purpose of task management is to make our lives more intentional

Task management can become a substitute for doing. We focus on planning, prioritising, describing, and re-planning the things we haven't done, with very little that has actually been done. Or we drop task management altogether to avoid the trap, and we fall in the trap of losing focus instead.

Fulfilling the potential of task management ultimately means doing the thing.

When used from within our task management tool, AI helps us knowing what to do, how to do it, and planning it almost instantly. Doing the thing finally becomes what we spend our time and energy on. Doing the work also gets a boost since we can get in-context feedback and help when we need it. And because we work inside the plan, our actions become more intentional.
